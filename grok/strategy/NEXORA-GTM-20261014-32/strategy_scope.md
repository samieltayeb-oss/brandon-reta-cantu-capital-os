# NEXORA-GTM-20261014-32 — Free Assessment Scope, Service Matching & Proposal Transition
Strategy Analyst · 2026-10-14 MT · Builds on GTM-29 A/B and GTM-26 01/04/05. **Everything here is PENDING CEO REVIEW.** No prices are approved or quoted. No outreach, publishing or website changes.

CEO-approved journey (2026-10-14): (1) FREE 20-minute intro consultation → (2) FREE focused Business Technology & Growth Assessment → (3) paid implementation proposal → (4) optional recurring support and optimization.

## 1. Free assessment scope
### Inputs (allowed only)
- The prospect's public website and public pages (services, forms, booking, portals visible without login).
- Public process observations (e.g., how a quote request is submitted, published contact routes).
- What the prospect voluntarily describes in the intro call or a short intake questionnaire: tools used by name, headcount band, the 1–2 workflows that hurt most, current manual steps.
- Not allowed: logins, system access, exports, screenshots of records, customer/employee/patient data, financials, contracts.

### Effort budget (max 90 min; target 60)
| Step | 60-min plan | 90-min ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Prep: review intro-call notes + intake answers | 10 | 15 |
| Public website/process review (forms, CTAs, visible tools) | 15 | 20 |
| Map up to 2 workflows (steps, handoffs, re-keying points) as described | 15 | 25 |
| Match findings to offers/services (§2); note evidence limits | 10 | 15 |
| Write ≤2-page summary + internal QA (claims check) | 10 | 15 |
Hard stop at 90 minutes. Anything more goes into the paid proposal's scope. Log actual minutes per assessment so the CEO gets real cost data.

### Outputs
- One findings summary of 2 pages or less (template below), delivered to the prospect after CEO-approved sending rules.
- An internal note: service match, qualification score (§3), time spent.

### Findings summary template (≤2 pages)
1. Header: company, date, workflows reviewed (max 2), sources used ("public website + what you told us on <date>").
2. What we heard: the prospect's own description of each workflow, in their words.
3. Opportunities (3–5 bullets): the issue observed or described → the type of improvement (e.g., "intake form not connected to scheduling; could be connected"). No ROI, hours-saved or cost figures.
4. Evidence limits: what we did not see (no system access; tool capabilities unverified; volumes unknown).
5. Suggested next steps: an optional paid proposal covering the named scope; what we would need to confirm.
6. Statement: "This is an observational summary, not a technical design or guarantee."

### Guardrails
- No architecture diagrams, code, integration builds, prototypes or configured demos.
- No invented ROI, savings, timelines or technical feasibility claims. Use "could", "typically" and "to be confirmed".
- Claims follow GTM-29 A §4 (proof-safe list). Never promise SummitOS availability or a WMS product.
- **If the prospect offers system access, logins, exports or sensitive data:** politely decline in writing ("For the free assessment we only use public information and what you describe; please don't send logins or records"); do not open, store or forward anything already sent; delete accidental attachments and note the deletion internally; tell the CEO. Access may only be considered later under a signed paid engagement with agreed data terms.
- Health information (clinics): no free assessments for clinic workflows that involve patient data until the privacy hold (GTM-26 D20) is resolved. Front-office website review only.
- Scope creep: a third workflow or technical questions → "We'll cover that in a proposal."
- No personal contact data captured beyond what the prospect gives for the engagement; follow CASL/PIPA rules per R&C.

## 3. Transition to a paid proposal
### Qualification criteria (all required unless the CEO waives one)
1. A specific workflow problem the prospect confirmed (not only one we inferred).
2. A named decision-maker took part, or confirmed they will review.
3. Fit with portfolio readiness: the solution uses LIVE services (not a demo or pilot).
4. Priority segment (3–75 employees, Calgary/Alberta) or a CEO-approved exception (D28).
5. The prospect agrees that next-step work is paid and will share system details under a signed engagement.
6. No privacy or legal blockers (e.g., health data hold).
### The proposal must contain
Problem statement in the client's words; scope and exclusions; deliverables and milestones; what NEXORA needs (access, contacts, data terms); assumptions and risks; success measures to be agreed (no promised figures); price and payment terms from the CEO-approved schedule only; the optional Offer 4 path; validity date; confidentiality and data-handling terms.
### Exit / no-fit outcomes
- **No fit:** send a short thank-you with the summary; optionally point to the free checklists. No further follow-up unless the prospect asks.
- **Not ready (timing or budget):** agreed check-in date only if the prospect requests it.
- **Needs a capability we don't offer** (e.g., SEO retainer, ERP implementation, WMS product): say so plainly. A referral only under a CEO-approved partner policy (none exists).
- **Too large or complex:** CEO decides on an exception (D28) or declines.
- **Sensitive-data dependency:** pause until terms or the privacy decision are in place.
