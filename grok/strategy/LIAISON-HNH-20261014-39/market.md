**Implication (H):** Shrinking temporary-resident targets cut student and work-permit volume. Stable PR targets and a near-exhausted AAIP allocation raise competition for nominations. Together these push consultancies toward PR, PNP, employer/LMIA and refusal/appeal work.

### Regulatory context (high level)
- **V:** CICC licenses and regulates RCICs and RISIAs under the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants Act. It runs a public register to verify licensees and a fraud-prevention campaign against unauthorized practitioners. https://rcic.ca/
- **V (general law; details not re-fetched):** Paid immigration advice or representation is restricted to authorized representatives: RCICs, lawyers or Quebec notaries who are members of a provincial law society, and paralegals in Ontario. Anyone else charging for it is an unauthorized representative under IRPA s.91.
- **RCIC vs lawyer (H/V):**
  - RCIC-IRB holders (2,076) can appear before the Immigration and Refugee Board.
  - Only lawyers can take matters to Federal Court for judicial review.
  - One competitor's fee page claims lawyers charge "$5,000-15,000 for equivalent work". That's a self-serving competitor claim (H).
