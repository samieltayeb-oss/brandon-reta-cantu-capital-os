## (b) Competitive positioning
### HNH-owned CRM vs CaseEasy's own features (overlap)
- The proposal: the "HNH-owned CRM, with CaseEasy retained" handles leads, bookings, consent and CEO reporting, while CaseEasy keeps cases, forms, portal, agreements and finance (V, S6).
- CaseEasy's documented overlap (V, strategy.md): prospects and "Prospect Assessments & Scoring"; Zapier prospect sync from web/social sources (C11); Calendly sync that creates prospects (C12); appointment scheduling and online booking (C2); triggers/autoresponder (C5); reporting across cases, clients, revenue and team (C9); email sync (C10).
- **Overlap zone (H):** lead capture, booking and basic follow-up are already advertised by CaseEasy. Clearer non-overlap value: WhatsApp channel and multilingual reception (not documented in CaseEasy's integration list — U), consent records, cross-system CEO reporting (marketing + CaseEasy + accounting), MENA campaign attribution.
- Risk (H): two systems of record for prospects means duplicate data, sync disputes and staff confusion, and the proposal itself concedes "listed… integrations do not establish full two-way synchronization" (V, S6n). Option: position the CRM as a **channel and attribution layer that hands qualified prospects into CaseEasy**, not a parallel client database.

### CaseEasy "cutover" risk
- V: S23 says "HNH approves the cutover only after staff acceptance and recovery tests" and "Keep CaseEasy active until the required replacement is proven. February delivery and cancellation are not promised."
- V: S27/S6n say "full CaseEasy replacement need[s] separate scope and pricing" and the CRM is "not full parity".
- **Assessment (H):** the words "cutover", "required replacement" and the February renewal "review checkpoint" imply a path to replacing at least some CaseEasy functions (prospects/CRM, possibly more). That **conflicts with the "around, not replacing" positioning** in strategy.md and GAP_ANALYSIS (C7). It also invites a head-on comparison with an incumbent that has 500+ firms, IRCC form autofill and trust accounting (V, CaseEasy site), and with CaseEasy Enterprise's own integration services.
- Further risk (H): migrating prospect/client history out of CaseEasy depends on export, which is undocumented publicly (C21 = U). That is an Argon dependency (A2, A7).
- Options for the CEO: (1) restate in follow-up material that the cutover means only lead/booking functions moving to the channel layer and that CaseEasy stays the case system; (2) drop cutover language; (3) keep it but require Argon's export validation first.

### UUDigital CA$30k benchmark: verification
- **Source verified (V):** https://uudigital.agency/services/crm-development returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-14 and shows "Custom CRM MVP From $30,000 CAD — Best for: A focused internal CRM for leads, clients, tasks, roles and reporting" and "Full custom CRM systems typically start from $30,000 CAD". The proposal quotes it accurately and checked it Oct 12 (V, S19).
- **Not like-for-like (H, with V evidence):** the same page also lists **"CRM Integrations from $5,000"** (connecting an existing CRM to forms, dashboards, automations) and **"CRM Dashboard Layer From $7,500 CAD"**. Because NEXORA's offer is positioned around CaseEasy (integration + dashboard + channel layer), those lower UUDigital tiers are arguably the closer comparison, not the $30k custom-build tier. The slide does say "Different defined scopes, not competing quotes" (V), but placing $30k next to CA$14,900 implies a value gap that a buyer who reads the page would see as selective.
- Option: in future material, cite the full UUDigital ladder ($5k / $7.5k / $30k) or drop the benchmark.
