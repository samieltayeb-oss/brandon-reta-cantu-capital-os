## 0. Citation corrections to the brief
- **C1. The "business-card exception" is s.10(9)(c), not s.10(10).** s.10(10) defines "existing business relationship", which is where the 2-year and 6-month windows come from. The disclosure-based implied consent (the usual business-card scenario) is s.10(9)(c).
- **C2. Competition Act s.74.01(1)(c) is not about testimonials.** The verified text covers representations that "purport to be (i) a warranty or guarantee of a product, or (ii) a promise to replace, maintain or repair…". Testimonials are addressed in **s.74.02**, verified text in §3.
- **C3. "Material connection" disclosure.** The text "material connection" and "endorse" do not appear in the local Competition Act text. Any disclosure duty for paid or connected endorsements is **UNVERIFIED** as statute. It may exist in Competition Bureau guidance, which I didn't retrieve, so it's for counsel.

## 1. CASL
- **L1. LEGAL REQUIREMENT (s.6(1)).** "It is prohibited to send or cause or permit to be sent to an electronic address a commercial electronic message unless (a) the person to whom the message is sent has consented to receiving it, whether the consent is express or implied; and (b) the message complies with subsection (2)."
- **L2. LEGAL REQUIREMENT (s.6(2)).** The message "must (a) set out prescribed information that identifies the person who sent the message and the person — if different — on whose behalf it is sent; (b) set out information enabling the person to whom the message is sent to readily contact one of the persons referred to in paragraph (a); and (c) set out an unsubscribe mechanism in accordance with subsection 11(1)." Under s.6(3), the contact information must be "valid for a minimum of 60 days after the message has been sent."
- **L3. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, express consent requests (s.10(1)).** The request must "set out clearly and simply": "(a) the purpose or purposes for which the consent is being sought; (b) prescribed information that identifies the person seeking consent…; and (c) any other prescribed information."
- **L4. LEGAL REQUIREMENT (SOR/2012-36 s.4).** A request "may be obtained orally or in writing and must be sought separately for each act described in sections 6 to 8 of the Act". It must include the business name, the on-whose-behalf identity, the mailing address plus phone, email or web address and, per CRTC Bulletin 2012-548 para 20, "a statement indicating that the person whose consent is sought can withdraw their consent" (para 4(e)).
- **L5. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, implied consent (s.10(9)).** Consent is implied only in these cases:
  - **(a)** an existing business or non-business relationship;
  - **(b)** conspicuous publication: the person "has conspicuously published, or has caused to be conspicuously published, the electronic address… the publication is not accompanied by a statement that the person does not wish to receive unsolicited commercial electronic messages… and the message is relevant to the person's business, role, functions or duties in a business or official capacity";
  - **(c)** disclosure: the person "has disclosed, to the person who sends the message… the electronic address… without indicating a wish not to receive unsolicited commercial electronic messages… and the message is relevant to the person's business, role, functions or duties…";
  - **(d)** circumstances set out in the regulations.
- **L6. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, windows (s.10(10)).** An existing business relationship arises from:
  - a purchase or lease "within the two-year period immediately before the day on which the message was sent" (also a written contract "currently in existence or expired within" that period);
  - or "an inquiry or application, within the six-month period immediately before the day on which the message was sent".

  Under s.10(14)(a), for subscriptions the period begins "on the day that the subscription, account, loan or other relationship terminates".
- **L7. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, unsubscribe (s.11).**
  - Under s.11(1), the mechanism must let the recipient opt out "at no cost to them", "using (i) the same electronic means by which the message was sent, or (ii) if using those means is not practicable, any other electronic means", and must "specify an electronic address, or link to a page on the World Wide Web".
  - Under s.11(3), effect must be given "without delay, and in any event no later than 10 business days after the indication has been sent".
  - Under SOR/2012-36 s.3, the identification and unsubscribe "must be set out clearly and prominently" and the mechanism "must be able to be readily performed".
- **L8. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, content (SOR/2012-36 s.2(1)).** Every commercial electronic message must set out:
  - (a) the name the sender carries on business under;
  - (b)–(c) the on-whose-behalf name and which party is which;
  - (d) "the mailing address, and either a telephone number providing access to an agent or a voice messaging system, an email address or a web address".
- **L9. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, referral (SOR/2013-221 s.4(1)).** s.6(1)(a) "does not apply to the first commercial electronic message that is sent… following a referral by any individual who has an existing business relationship, an existing non-business relationship, a family relationship or a personal relationship with the person who sends the message as well as any of those relationships with the individual to whom the message is sent and that discloses the full name of the individual or individuals who made the referral and states that the message is sent as a result of the referral."
- **L10. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, records (s.13).** "A person who alleges that they have consent… has the onus of proving it."
- **I1. INTERPRETATION.** Only the **first** referral message is exempt. The referrer must have a qualifying relationship with **both** parties, and the s.6(2) content and unsubscribe rules still apply (the exemption covers s.6(1)(a) only). Later messages need consent.
- **I2. INTERPRETATION.** Publicly available business information is not consent by itself. All three s.10(9)(b) conditions must be met for each address, and a published general inbox or media-contact address may fail the relevance test.
- **I3. INTERPRETATION (CRTC guidance, not statute).**
  - **Pre-checked boxes:** Bulletin 2012-548 para 18 treats as compliant "a separate tick-box… which must be proactively checked by the person whose consent is being sought" (it refers to Bulletin 2012-549). Pre-checked boxes are therefore not a means the CRTC lists as compliant.
  - **No bundling:** para 16 says consent requests "must not be subsumed in, or bundled with, requests for consent to the general terms and conditions".
  - **Records:** para 26 gives as an acceptable written means a checkbox "where a record of the date, time, purpose, and manner of that consent is stored in a database".
- **I4. INTERPRETATION.** Whether LinkedIn InMail, connection notes or social direct messages are messages "to an electronic address" under CASL is not settled in the texts reviewed and is for counsel. Earlier reviews (LIAISON-07) made the same point. Organic public posts are generally not messages sent to an electronic address. **That last point is an INTERPRETATION; confirm it with counsel.**

## 2. PIPA (Alberta) and PIPEDA: lead collection
- **L11. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, consent (PIPA s.7(1)).** An organization "shall not… (a) collect that information unless the individual consents to the collection". **PIPEDA Sch.1 4.3.6:** "An organization should generally seek express consent when the information is likely to be considered sensitive."
- **L12. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, purpose notice.**
  - **PIPA s.13(1):** "Before or at the time of collecting personal information… an organization must notify that individual in writing or orally (a) as to the purposes for which the information is collected, and (b) of the name or position name or title of a person who is able to answer… questions about the collection."
  - **PIPEDA 4.2.3:** "The identified purposes should be specified at or before the time of collection."
- **L13. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, limiting collection.**
  - **PIPA s.11(1):** "may collect personal information only for purposes that are reasonable."
  - **PIPEDA 4.4:** "The collection of personal information shall be limited to that which is necessary for the purposes identified by the organization."
- **L14. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, retention.**
  - **PIPA s.35(1):** "may retain personal information only for as long as the organization reasonably requires the personal information for legal or business purposes". Under s.35(2), within a reasonable period after that, it must destroy the records or make the information non-identifying.
  - **PIPEDA 4.5.3:** information no longer required "should be destroyed, erased, or made anonymous".
- **L15. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, cross-border processors.**
  - **PIPA s.13.1(1)–(3):** an organization using "a service provider outside Canada" to collect, or transferring information to one, must "before or at the time of collecting or transferring" notify "(a) the way in which the individual may obtain access to written information about the organization's policies and practices with respect to service providers outside Canada, and (b) the name or position name or title of a person who is able to answer…". Under s.13.1(4), this is in addition to the s.13 notice.
  - **PIPEDA 4.1.3:** the organization "is responsible for personal information… including information that has been transferred to a third party for processing" and "shall use contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection".
- **L16. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, business-contact carve-out.** PIPEDA s.4.01 excludes business contact information collected "solely for the purpose of communicating or facilitating communication with the individual in relation to their employment, business or profession". PIPA s.4(3)(d) is the equivalent (verified in earlier reviews).
- **I5. INTERPRETATION.** Lead scoring, enrichment, profiling or ad-audience building go beyond "solely for communicating", so the carve-out probably doesn't cover them. Treat that data as personal information.
- **I6. INTERPRETATION.** US-hosted CRM, form, webinar, email or ad tools are probably "service providers outside Canada", which would trigger the PIPA s.13.1 notice for Alberta leads.

## 3. Competition Act: marketing claims and testimonials
- **L17. LEGAL REQUIREMENT.** s.74.01(1)(a) makes it reviewable conduct to make "a representation to the public that is false or misleading in a material respect". s.52(1) is the criminal counterpart (verified in earlier reviews).
- **L18. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, testing.** s.74.01(1)(b): a performance claim "that is not based on an adequate and proper test thereof, the proof of which lies on the person making the representation".
- **L19. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, general impression.** s.52(4): "the general impression conveyed by a representation as well as its literal meaning shall be taken into account". The Act has a parallel general-impression provision for s.74.01.
- **L20. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, guarantees.** s.74.01(1)(c) covers representations that "purport to be (i) a warranty or guarantee of a product, or (ii) a promise to replace, maintain or repair" (see C2 for the rest of the paragraph). This applies to any uptime, ROI or "guaranteed" wording.
- **L21. LEGAL REQUIREMENT, testimonials (s.74.02).** Publishing "a testimonial with respect to a product" is reviewable "unless the person… can establish that (a) such a representation or testimonial was previously made or published by the person by whom the… testimonial was given, or (b)… before being made or published, approved and permission to make or publish it was given in writing by the person by whom… the testimonial was given". The rest of the provision was cut off in the extract and should be read in full.
- **UNVERIFIED.** A statutory duty to disclose material connections (paid or incentivized reviews, referral-fee partners) wasn't found in the text. Refer it to counsel and the Competition Bureau's guidance.
- **I7. INTERPRETATION.** The following are NEXORA's own representations: "AI" capability claims, time-saved or ROI figures, "secure" or "Canadian-hosted" claims, and case studies. The tier claims flagged in LIAISON-06 (Foundation vs Live Intelligence) carry over.

## 4. Lead magnets and assessment forms
- **I8. INTERPRETATION (CRTC 2012-548 paras 16 and 18).** Marketing consent should be a separate, **unchecked** box that's distinct from the terms of use and from the download or assessment delivery.
- **R1. RECOMMENDATION.** Collect the minimum: name, business email, company and role. Make phone, size or budget optional, and only where the stated purpose needs them.
  - Show the purpose (PIPA s.13 / PIPEDA 4.2.3), the contact person and the s.13.1 notice on the form.
  - Store the consent record: timestamp, form version, consent wording, IP or source, and purpose.
  - Delivering an asset someone requested doesn't need a marketing opt-in. Follow-up promotional messages rely on express consent, or on implied consent through the 6-month inquiry window, which must be tracked.

## 6. Pre-outreach compliance checklist (sales playbook)
Every item must be answered before the first commercial electronic message.
1. ☐ The CEO has approved the account and the channel. Nothing in research files is approval.
2. ☐ Consent basis recorded per address. One of:
   - express consent (source, date, wording);
   - s.10(9)(b) (published URL, capture date, no-solicitation statement checked, role relevance);
   - s.10(9)(c) disclosure;
   - a business relationship within 2 years, or an inquiry within 6 months (with the date);
   - a referral (referrer's full name, and confirmation of their relationships with both parties).
3. ☐ No guessed or derived addresses, and no personal or freemail addresses unless the person disclosed them.
4. ☐ The message identifies NEXORA Technologies Corp., has a mailing address plus phone, email or web contact valid for 60 days or more, and has a working, clearly placed unsubscribe.
5. ☐ The unsubscribe process honors requests within 10 business days, and a suppression list is checked before sending.
6. ☐ Referral messages: the referrer's full name is given, the message says it was sent because of the referral, and it is the first message only.
7. ☐ Every claim is on the approved claims list with substantiation. No guarantees, ROI or uptime figures without tests or terms (ss.74.01(1)(b)–(c)). Testimonials have written permission (s.74.02).
8. ☐ No third-party prospect claims are repeated (the do-not-repeat lists).
9. ☐ Privacy: only minimal fields are stored, the retention date is set (PIPA s.35), and processors outside Canada are disclosed in the privacy notice (s.13.1).
10. ☐ Clinic or health-sector accounts: hold until counsel reviews.
11. ☐ LinkedIn or social direct messages: wait for counsel's view (I4), and use no automation.
