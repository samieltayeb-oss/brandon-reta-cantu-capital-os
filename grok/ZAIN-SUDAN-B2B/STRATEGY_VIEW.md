## 2. Each sector in plain words
For each sector: **Why it works / Why Zain / Best offers / Difficulty.**

**1. Banking / fintech.** *Why it works:* Cash is scarce and the currency is collapsing (F12), so digital money is now a necessity. Eight wallets are licensed (F3, F4), and every bank and wallet needs SMS OTP, USSD and payout rails. *Why Zain:* Zain owns the SIM, SMPP/USSD and Bede (F2, F3), and it reaches 12.4M customers (F1). *Offers:* A2P OTP over SMPP, USSD menus for banks, a Bede payouts and collections API, and a merchant QR rollout. *Difficulty:* Medium-high. Bede competes with MoMo and SAH (F4), so other wallets may refuse to use Bede's rails. CBoS rules and KYC apply. The parallel rate gap (F12) distorts pricing.

**2. NGOs / UN.** *Why it works:* Humanitarian groups have to pay people, reach them and keep their staff connected in a country where cash and networks fail (F11). Bede already markets fund distribution "for NGOs" (F3). *Why Zain:* A large, trusted, licensed brand that gives donors an audit trail, and that the Port Sudan authorities talk to (F7). *Offers:* a "Connected Aid" bundle: bulk disbursement with reconciliation reports, a beneficiary SMS/IVR feedback hotline, staff SIM pools with APN, and an outage fallback plan. *Difficulty:* High on procurement. UN agencies use formal tenders, vendor rosters and strict data protection and neutrality rules. Their policies on serving areas held by each side differ. Starlink is their default backup (F10). Speed depends on joining existing cash-working groups (UNKNOWN process).

**3. Government.** *Why it works:* The Eisali SMS deal (F5) and the customs IoT deal (F6) show government is already buying. *Why Zain:* National carrier relationships plus ministerial goodwill (F7). *Offers:* the notification and e-collection SMS template rolled out to tax, customs, passports and civil registry; citizen IVR/CCaaS; Bede bill payment. *Difficulty:* Slow payment and payment in SDG, political exposure (two rival "governments", F10), and the 2027 licence renegotiation (F14) adding leverage both ways. Do not touch anything security-related.

**4. Retail / SMEs and relocated businesses.** *Why it works:* Businesses moving to Port Sudan, the east, the north, Egypt and the Gulf, or returning to Khartoum, need SIMs, payments and a way to reach customers fast (ESTIMATE, consistent with the "return of population" in F1 and F8). *Why Zain:* Flex business bundles, POS and Bede merchant products are already listed (F2). *Offers:* a "Business in a box" bundle, and an Arabic WhatsApp/AI front desk built by FP. *Difficulty:* Low ticket size and high churn. SDG pricing loses value monthly. This needs channel sales (Zain's Authorized Sales Partner program, F2), not direct sales.

**5. Logistics / ports.** *Why it works:* Port Sudan is the working gateway (ESTIMATE), and trucking moves everything inland. *Why Zain:* Fleet Management and VTS are already offered (F2), and the customs/Jointech deal (F6) puts Zain in the cargo flow. *Offers:* truck tracking plus fuel and driver payouts through Bede. *Difficulty:* Jointech may already own the customs layer (F6), so FP must complement it, not compete. Road security and coverage gaps.

**8. Mining (gold).** *Why it works:* Gold is a major hard-currency earner (ESTIMATE; figures UNKNOWN here), and remote sites need connectivity. *Why Zain:* Private APN and payroll. *Offers:* site connectivity, worker payroll on Bede, asset/IoT tracking. *Difficulty:* **High reputational and sanctions risk.** Gold is tied to armed-group financing, and E.O. 14098 designations (F13) can hit counterparties. Strict screening is required; I would only do it with Zain-led due diligence. Low on brand fit for that reason.

**6. Health.** *Why:* clinics and NGOs running health programmes need reminders and payments to health workers. *Why Zain:* Digital health came up in the ministerial meeting (F7). *Offers:* SMS reminders and connectivity. *Difficulty:* donor-dependent, and health data sensitivity.

**7. Agriculture.** *Why:* the largest employer (ESTIMATE), and the rural reach of USSD fits. *Offers:* advisory SMS, input payments. *Difficulty:* seasonal, low ability to pay, and best sold through NGOs or the FAO (so it overlaps with sector 2).

**10. Education.** Exam result SMS is a known pattern (ESTIMATE). Difficulty: exam shutdowns (F11) and low budgets.

**11. Oil / energy.** Private APN/SCADA. Difficulty: assets sit in contested areas, sales cycles are long, and specialist security is needed.

**9. Construction / reconstruction.** Large later on, but it depends on peace and donor reconstruction funds (ESTIMATE). Prepare offers now and sell later.

**12. Hospitality.** Small and slow to recover. Keep it in the SME bundle.

## 4. Phased plan (organised by the sector ranking)
**0 to 90 days (prove it):**

- Build one demo: a Bede bulk-disbursement reconciliation portal with SMS confirmations and an IVR feedback line, in Arabic and English, working offline-tolerant.
- Get one paid or no-cost pilot with a single NGO or bank through Zain. Go/no-go: Zain signs a partner agreement and one pilot customer is live.

**3 to 6 months (repeat):**

**6 to 12 months (scale):**

## 5. Franco Pinto partnership model
- **What FP builds:**
  - integration and middleware (Bede/SMPP/USSD APIs to client ERPs and NGO beneficiary systems);
  - a reconciliation and reporting portal;
  - Arabic AI/WhatsApp front desk and IVR flows;
  - dashboards for fleet and IoT.
- **What FP does not build:** network, licensing or wallet core. Zain keeps all of these.
- **How FP earns (all ESTIMATE structures, with no prices because there is no approved schedule and no market price was found):**
  1. **Setup/integration fee** per enterprise client, invoiced in USD offshore.
  2. **White-label SaaS licence** per client per month, sold under the Zain brand.
  3. **Managed service retainer** (support, monitoring, outage runbooks).
  4. **Revenue share** on incremental Zain traffic (SMS and Bede transaction fees) FP brings in. This needs a clear attribution clause.
- **Preference:** fees paid by Zain (or Zain Group entities) in USD outside Sudan, rather than collected from Sudanese end clients in SDG.
- **Payment and collection risks:**
  - SDG devaluation: 600 to about 7,500 per USD, with a large official/parallel gap (F12). Any fee in SDG loses value fast.
  - Repatriating hard currency from Sudan may be slow or blocked (UNKNOWN on current CBoS rules).
  - Pass-through revenue share pays late if it depends on Zain Sudan's local cash.
  - Use milestone payments, prepayment for setup, USD pricing indexed in the contract, and a cap on receivables.

## 6. Difficulty and risks (honest)
- **Security:** active war; staff cannot travel to many areas. Work remotely; host data outside conflict zones (data residency rules are UNKNOWN; Risk to check).
- **Payments and FX:** see §5. This is the biggest commercial risk.
- **Sanctions:** Sudan is not under a broad embargo (F13), but E.O. 14098 and 13400 designations hit individuals, RSF- and SAF-linked firms and companies in gold and arms. Screen every counterparty. Avoid RSF-held territory deals and the "Government of Peace" (F10). Canadian sanctions on Sudan also apply to NEXORA and FP if they are Canadian persons (UNKNOWN details; Legal to check).
- **Outages:** exam shutdowns and conflict outages (F11). Every offer needs offline and retry design, and possibly a satellite fallback. But Starlink is unlicensed under Port Sudan rules (F10), so Zain cannot bundle it.
- **Zain procurement:** Zain Group is large (F1), and ZainTECH plus existing vendors (e.g. Jointech, F6) may be preferred. Vendor registration, security review and Group approvals are likely slow (ESTIMATE; process UNKNOWN).
- **Relationship dependence:** this pitch rests on personal access to Zain B2B leadership. If that contact moves, the pipeline stops. The partner agreement should name the program, not the person.
- **Conflict of interest:** Sami is both NEXORA founder and FP manager. Who owns the IP and the revenue must be decided before the pitch.
- **Competitors:** MTN (MoMo) and Sudani (SAH) are recovering strongly (F8, F9) and could copy bundles quickly. Integration depth and NGO references are the moat (ESTIMATE).
